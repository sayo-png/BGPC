Learn more about the different bugs we treat

Ants

Description: Ants are small insects that live in colonies and are commonly found both indoors and outdoors. They often enter homes in search of food, water, and shelter.

Appearance: Small insects with three distinct body segments, six legs, and elbowed antennae. Color range from black and brown to reddish.

Common Habitats: Kitchens, bathrooms, gardens, lawns, and foundations

Signs of Infestation: Visible ant trails, small piles of dirt, ants near food sources, and nests around the property.

Potential Risks: Certain species can contaminate food, cause painful bites, or damage wood structures.

Prevention: Keep food sealed, clean up spills, eliminate standing water, and seal cracks around doors and windows.

Cockroaches

Description: Cockroaches are resilient insects that thrive in warm, moist environments.

Appearance: Flat, oval-shaped bodies with long antennae and six legs. They are typically brown, reddish-brown, or black.

Common Habitats: Kitchens, bathrooms, basements, drains, cabinets, and behind appliances.

Signs of Infestation: Droppings, egg cases, shed skins, unpleasant odors, and live roaches.

Potential Risks: Can spread bacteria, contaminate food, and trigger allergies or asthma symptoms.

Prevention: Keep surfaces clean, store food properly, fix plumbing leaks, and remove clutter

Spiders

Description: Spiders are eight-legged arachnids that feed on insects and other small pests. While most spiders are harmless to humans, certain species can deliver medically significant bites.

Appearance: Eight legs, two main body sections, and multiple eyes. Colors and sizes vary depending on the species.

Common Habitats: Basements, attics, garages, closets, corners, and outdoor vegetation.

Signs of Infestation: Spider webs, egg sacs, frequent spider sightings, and increased insect activity.

Potential Risks: Most spiders are not dangerous, but bites from certain species, such as black widows and brown recluses, can require medical attention.

Prevention: Reduce clutter, seal entry points, remove webs, and control other insects that serve as food sources.

Silverfish

Description: Silverfish are small, wingless insects that prefer dark, damp environments. They feed on materials containing starches and carbohydrates and can damage household belongings.

Appearance: Silver or gray, elongated bodies with three tail-like appendages and long antennae.

Common Habitats: Bathrooms, basements, attics, closets, kitchens, and storage areas

Signs of Infestation: Small holes in paper, books, wallpaper, cardboard, and clothing, along with shed skins and droppings

Potential Risks: They do not bite or transmit diseases to humans, but can damage book, documents, clothing, and other belongings.

Prevention: Reduce indoor humidity, repair leaks, store paper products properly, and keep storage areas organized.

Crickets

Description: Crickets are jumping insects known for their distinctive chirping sounds. They are generally outdoor pests but can enter homes seeking food, warmth, and shelter.

Appearance: Typically brown, black, or tan with long antennae, powerful hind legs, and six legs.

Common Habitats: Gardens, lawns, basements, garages, crawl spaces, and areas around foundations.

Signs of Infestation: Chriping sounds, visible crickets, droppings, and damage to fabrics or stored materials.

Potential Risks: Generally harmless to humans, but some species can damage fabrics, plants, and household materials.

Prevention: Seal gaps around doors and windows, reduce outdoor lighting near entryways, and elimate excess moisture.

Bees

Description: Bees are beneficial pollinating insects that play an important role in maintaining healthy ecosystems. However, nests located near homes and high-traffic areas can create safety concerns.

Appearance: Fuzzy-bodied insects with six legs, two pairs of wings, and colors commonly ranging from yellow and black to brown.

Common Habitats: Gardens, trees, wall cavities, roof spaces, and other proctect outdoor areas.

Signs of Infestation: Increased bee activity around a particular area, visible swarms, and buzzing sounds near walls or roofs.

Potential Risks: Bee stings can cause pain, swelling, and allergic reactions. Severe allergic reactions require emergency medial attention.

Prevention: Inspect potential nesting areas, seal appropriate structural openings when inactive, and avoid disturbing established colonies.

Wasps

Description: Wasps are flying insects that can become aggressive when their nests are disturbed. They are commonly found around homes, gardens, and outdoor structures.

Appearance: Slender bodies, narrow waists, six legs, and two pairs of wings. Many species have yellow and black markings.

Common Habitats: Roof eaves, attics, wall cavities, trees, shurbs, and underground nests

Signs of Infestation: Visible nests, increased wasp activity, buzzing near strucutres, and repeated sightings in one area.

Potential Risks: Wasps can deliver painful stings and may string multiple times. Stings can cause serious allergic reactions in some individuals.

Prevention: Keep garbage covered, limit exposed food outdoors, seal appropriate entry points, and regularly inspect outdoor structures.

Rodents (Mice & Rats)

Description: Rodents, including mice and rats, are common household pests that enter buildings in search of food, water, and shelter. They reproduce quickly and can cause significant property damage.

Appearance: Small to medium-size mammals with fur, prominent front teeth, long tails, and strong shewing abilities.

Common Habitats: Attics, basements, wall cavities, garages, kitchens, and storage areas.

Signs of Infestation: Droppings, gnaw marks scratching sounds, nesting materials, damagaed food packaging, and greasy marks along walls.

Potential Risks: Rodents can spread diseases, contaminate food, damage property, and chew electrical wiringm potentially creating fire hazards.

Prevention: Store food securely, seal holes and gaps, maintain clean spaces, and remove potential nesting material.

Bed Bugs

Description: Bed bugs are small, parasitic insects that feed on human blood, typically while people are sleeping. They can spread through luggage, furniture, clothing, and other personal belongings.

Appearance: Small, flat, oval-shaped insects that are reddish-brown as adults. Young bed bugs are smaller and lighter in color.

Common Habitats: Mattress seams, bed frames, headboards, furniture, cracks, and crevices near sleeping areas.

Signs of Infestation: Small blood spots on beeding, dark fecal stains, shed skins, eggs, and live bugs.

Potential Risks: Beg bugs do not typically transmit diseases, but their bites can cause itching, skin irritation, allergic reactions, and sleep disturbances.

Prevention: Inspect secondhand furniture, check hotel bedding and luggage, and avoid bringing potentially infested items to your home.

Fleas

Description: Fleas are tiny, blood-feeding parasites that commonly infest pets and househol environments. They reproduce quickly and can spread throughout carpets, furniture, and pet bedding.

Appearance: Small, dark reddish-brown insects with flattened bodies and powerful hind legs that allow them to jump.

Common Habitats: Pet fur, carpets, rights, furniture, pet bedding, and outdoor areas frequented by animals.

Signs of Infestation: Frequent scratching in pets, visible fleas, flea dirt (small dark specks), and itchy bites around the ankle.

Potential Risks: Fleas can cause allergic reactions, skin irritation, and transmit certain diseases and parasites.

Prevention: Regularly treat pets with veterinarian-approved flea prevention, vacuum frequently, and wash pet bedding

Ticks

Description: Ticks are small, blood-feeding arachnids commonly found in grassy, wooded, and brush-covered areas. They attach to humans and animals to feed and can transmit disease.

Appearance: Small, oval-shaped arachnids with eight legs as adults. Their color ranges from reddish-brown to dark brown or black.

Common Habitats: Tall grass, wooded areas, lead, litter, shrubgs, and places frequented by wildlife.

Signs of Infestation: Ticks found on pets, clothing, or skin, as well as incrased tick activity around oudoor areas.

Potential Risks: Ticks can transmit illnesses such as Lyme diseas, Rocky Mountain spotted fever, and other tick-borne diseases, depending on the species and location.

Prevention: Use EPA-registered repellents, wear protective clothing, check your body and pets after outdoor activities. and maintain lawns and vegetation.
